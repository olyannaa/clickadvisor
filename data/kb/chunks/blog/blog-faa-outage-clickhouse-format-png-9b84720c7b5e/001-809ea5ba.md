# The day the sky emptied: rendering the FAA outage with ClickHouse FORMAT PNG

![alexey-milovidov.webp](/_next/image?url=%2Fuploads%2Falexey_milovidov_0b4e074704.webp&w=96&q=75)[Alexey Milovidov](/authors/alexey-milovidov)Nov 18, 2026 · 17 minutes readOn Friday, November 13, 2026, an FAA outage grounded air traffic across the United States. This post shows the whole day over the lower 48 states, one frame per five minutes, from the ADS\-B data on [adsb.exposed](https://adsb.exposed/).

Loading video...The animation is rendered by ClickHouse itself. There is no plotting library and no image\-processing script. A SQL query returns one row per pixel, and the built\-in `PNG` output format turns those rows into an animated PNG. The map, the title, the clock and the timeline at the bottom are all rows of the same result set. Even the letters are SQL: a 3×5 bitmap font stored in a `map` literal.

The same animation is also available as an [animated PNG](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ClickHouse/adsb.exposed/faa-outage-2026-11-13/faa-outage-2026-11-13/faa-outage-2026-11-13.png), a [GIF](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ClickHouse/adsb.exposed/faa-outage-2026-11-13/faa-outage-2026-11-13/faa-outage-2026-11-13.gif) and an [MP4](/uploads/faa_outage_2026_11_13_57ddad198a.mp4).

## What the data shows \#

In the animation, each aircraft appears as a short streak: the distance it covered in five minutes. The colors follow the default palette of adsb.exposed:

- Red fades with altitude.
- Green grows over the first 5,000 feet, so climbs and descents around airports look yellow and orange.
- Blue shows ground speed, so the cruising traffic at high altitude is blue.

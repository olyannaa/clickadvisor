The [full benchmark definition, accepted runs, results, cost summaries, and reproduction steps](https://github.com/ClickHouse/CostBench/tree/main/full-path-realtime/quotes) are open in the CostBench repository. ## The gap opens while the data is still arriving \#

As the dataset grew toward 113\.2 billion rows, the systems kept preparing fresh quotes and answering queries. The three charts below show what accumulated along the way: fresh\-data\-path cost, normalized query\-serving cost, and query runtime.

ClickHouse Cloud stays near the bottom in all three. Across the accepted comparisons, it spent less keeping incoming data query\-ready, less serving the measured queries, and less time answering them. Its advantage was already visible while ingestion was running.

Those three results are connected. Preparing data efficiently carries a direct cost, but it also changes the work left for the query engine. CostBench combines preparation cost, query\-serving cost, and query runtime into one score to show their overall effect.

## Turning runtime and cost into performance per dollar \#

A fast query is useful; delivering it economically while new data keeps arriving is the full challenge. CostBench combines the three inputs shown above:

1. **Fresh\-data\-path cost:** ingestion, layout maintenance, and pre\-aggregation during the complete ingest.
2. **Normalized query\-serving cost:** the measured query workload priced at the applicable read\-side compute rates.
3. **Accumulated query runtime:** the time spent answering accepted queries while ingestion continued.

The score multiplies combined cost by accumulated query runtime. Lower cost improves the score; faster queries improve it too. Each provider’s result is compared with its matched ClickHouse baseline.

> The score rewards systems that keep incoming data query\-ready economically and serve the workload quickly. Lower is better.

Database storage costs are excluded from the score because their impact is small over the benchmark’s duration; see the calculation note below.

SCORE CALCULATION AND METERING NORMALIZATION (click to expand)

**WHAT THE FULL\-PATH SCORE MEANS**

 The score answers one question:  

**Where do you get the most full\-path real\-time performance per dollar spent?**

 It combines the cost of keeping continuously arriving data query\-ready with the cost and total runtime of serving the same query schedule. A lower score means a better combination of lower end\-to\-end cost and faster query serving under continuous ingestion.
 

**CALCULATION**

 End\-to\-end score \= (fresh\-data\-path cost \+ normalized query\-serving cost) × total query runtime. Lower is better.

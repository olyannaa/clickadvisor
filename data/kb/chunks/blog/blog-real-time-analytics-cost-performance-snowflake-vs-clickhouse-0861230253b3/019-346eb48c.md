Both ClickHouse Cloud and Snowflake store persisted data on low\-cost object storage. In this workload, the monthly storage bill is small compared with the cost of continuously ingesting data, maintaining query\-ready layouts, refreshing derived data, and serving queries.

The chart below shows the measured storage footprint after the first **31\.4 hours** of continuous ingest. At that point, each system had ingested roughly **113 billion stock\-quotes rows** at **1 million rows per second**, or about **75 MB per second**.

![Blog-Snowflake-response-02.001.png](/_next/image?url=%2Fuploads%2FBlog_Snowflake_response_02_001_91bf03b326.png&w=2048&q=75)
*Measurement queries: [ClickHouse](https://github.com/ClickHouse/CostBench/blob/main/full-path-realtime/quotes/clickhouse-cloud/results/storage-size-raw.md), [Snowflake](https://github.com/ClickHouse/CostBench/blob/main/full-path-realtime/quotes/snowflake/ops/collect_storage.sh).*

The chart uses the current list storage prices for the tested region: [$25\.30 per TB\-month for ClickHouse Cloud](https://clickhouse.com/pricing) and [$23\.00 per TB\-month for Snowflake](https://www.snowflake.com/en/pricing-options/) on AWS US East.

**ClickHouse raw data:**  
[362 GiB](https://github.com/ClickHouse/CostBench/blob/7f55e45e8f2ff45149af007d1098df87acb884f3/full-path-realtime/quotes/snowflake/results/t1/storage.json) × 2^30 / 10^12 × $25\.30 \= $9\.83/month

**Snowflake standard raw data:**  
[701 GiB](https://github.com/ClickHouse/CostBench/blob/7f55e45e8f2ff45149af007d1098df87acb884f3/full-path-realtime/quotes/snowflake/results/t1/storage.json) × 2^30 / 10^12 × $23\.00 \= $17\.31/month

**Snowflake Interactive raw data:**  
[2\.9 TiB](https://github.com/ClickHouse/CostBench/blob/7f55e45e8f2ff45149af007d1098df87acb884f3/full-path-realtime/quotes/snowflake/results/t1/storage.json) × 2^40 / 10^12 × $23\.00 \= $74\.12/month

The same calculation is used for the pre\-aggregated tables.

This also shows a notable storage\-side effect of Snowflake Interactive Tables.

> The raw Interactive Table is about **4\.2× larger** than the Snowflake standard raw table, and about **8\.2× larger** than the ClickHouse raw table.

That is consistent with Snowflake’s [documentation](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/interactive#cost-and-billing-considerations):

**"Interactive tables may be larger than equivalent standard tables, due to differences in data encoding and additional indexes."**

If the same ingest rate continued for a full 730\-hour month, the end\-of\-month storage footprint would scale roughly as follows:

| Data path | After 31\.4h | Cost at that size | Projected after 730h | Cost at projected size |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ClickHouse raw data | 362 GiB | $9\.83/mo | \~8\.2 TiB | \~$229/mo |
| Snowflake standard raw data | 701 GiB | $17\.31/mo | \~15\.9 TiB | \~$402/mo |
| Snowflake Interactive raw data | 2\.9 TiB | $74\.12/mo | \~67\.4 TiB | \~$1,724/mo |


Note that we don't know if the interactive table storage size grows linearly. We just assume it does.

Still, the absolute storage cost remains small compared with the compute and refresh costs measured in CostBench. The full\-path cost\-performance gap is driven by the running system: ingest compute, clustering or refresh work, and query execution while fresh data keeps arriving.

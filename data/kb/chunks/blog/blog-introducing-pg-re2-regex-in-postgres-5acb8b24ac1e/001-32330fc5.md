# Introducing pg\_re2, fast, RE2\-powered regular expressions in Postgres

![image 512x512 1](/_next/image?url=%2Fuploads%2FImage_512x512_1_8bc569c360.png&w=96&q=75)![image from slack 3](/_next/image?url=%2Fuploads%2FImage_from_Slack_3_99e6dde6b4.png&w=96&q=75)[David Wheeler](/authors/david-wheeler) and [Philip Dubé](/authors/philip-dube)Jul 8, 2026 · 10 minutes readSpeed is a feature that motivates much of ClickHouse product design and
engineering. One can plainly see this attention to performance in our
development of benchmarks, such as [ClickBench](https://benchmark.clickhouse.com "ClickBench — a Benchmark For Analytical DBMS") and [PostgresBench](https://postgresbench.clickhouse.com "PostgresBench — A Reproducible Benchmark for Postgres Services"), some of
which have become industry standards. Unsurprisingly this ethos reaches the
deepest crevices of our products, down to SQL functions and operators.

In that spirit, we've introduced [pg\_re2](https://pgxn.org/dist/re2/ "re2 extension on PGXN"), a Postgres extension providing fast
[RE2](https://github.com/google/re2/wiki/Syntax "RE2 Syntax")\-powered [regular expressions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_expression "Wikipedia: Regular expression") in Postgres. Install it from
[PGXN](https://pgxn.org/dist/re2/ "re2 extension on PGXN") or [GitHub](https://github.com/ClickHouse/pg_re2 "re2 extension on GitHub"); [ClickHouse Managed Postgres](https://clickhouse.com/docs/cloud/managed-postgres "ClickHouse Docs: Managed Postgres") already has it.

You can install it like so:

```
1CREATE EXTENSION re2;
```

Hit [the docs](https://pgxn.org/dist/re2/doc/re2.html "re2 extension Documentation") for the full list of features.

Of course, Postgres itself provides a wealth of [regular expression operators
and functions](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/functions-matching.html#FUNCTIONS-POSIX-REGEXP "PostgreSQL Docs: POSIX Regular Expressions"). Why provide an alternate? Two reasons: speed, of course,
but also compatibility.

## The need for speed \#

Ultimately, the Postgres and RE2 regular expression engines rest on
fundamentally different search algorithms. Postgres [POSIX](https://www.regular-expressions.info/posix.html "POSIX Basic Regular Expressions") regular
expressions depend on [backtracking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backtracking "Wikipedia: Backtracking"), returning the longest match, while RE2
relies on [finite automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite-state_machine "Wikipedia: Finite-state machine"). In his [article announcing RE2](https://swtch.com/~rsc/regexp/regexp3.html "Regular Expression Matching in the Wild") back in 2010,
[Russ Cox](https://swtch.com/~rsc/ "Russ Cox") summarized:

- The `gin_re2_ops` GIN opclass benefits from collecting the byte\-equivalent

expressions against the `pg_trgm` opclass fall back to a full table scan.
- On **plain\-word patterns** the RE2 `gin_re2_ops` and Postgres `pg_trgm`

Overall, the performance improvements identified by Russ Cox hold true for
regular expressions in Postgres, too.

## We are so compatible \#

The second driver for creating pg\_re2 was compatibility with ClickHouse
regular expression functions. Turns out that [RE2](https://github.com/google/re2/wiki/Syntax "RE2 Syntax") differs from [Postgres
POSIX expressions](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/functions-matching.html#FUNCTIONS-POSIX-REGEXP "PostgreSQL Docs: POSIX Regular Expressions") not only in speed but also syntax. These variations
matter because [pg\_clickhouse v0\.2\.0](https://github.com/ClickHouse/pg_clickhouse/releases/tag/v0.2.0 "pg_clickhouse v0.2.0 on GitHub") added [pushdown](https://whatisdatabase.com/optimizing-database-performance-with-pushdown-techniques "Optimizing Database Performance with Pushdown Techniques") for several PostgreSQL
operators and functions. It works well for simple regexen:

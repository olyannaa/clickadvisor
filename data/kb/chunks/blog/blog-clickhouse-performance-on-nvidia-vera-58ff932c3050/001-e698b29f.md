# Running ClickHouse on NVIDIA Vera CPU: Is your favorite database up to the challenge?

![robert schulze](/_next/image?url=%2Fuploads%2Frobert_schulze_fc3979ae99.png&w=96&q=75)[Robert Schulze](/authors/robert-schulze)Oct 21, 2026 · 14 minutes readThe engineers at ClickHouse are obsessed with performance. With every release, we look for new ways to make ClickHouse run faster and scale better on the underlying hardware. When NVIDIA recently provided us with an early\-access server of their upcoming Vera CPU platform, we did the obvious thing: we ran ClickBench, our popular benchmark for analytics, on it. We used the standard ClickHouse binary from our website and did not perform any optimizations or customizations in the database specifically for NVIDIA Vera. [The results speak for themselves](https://x.com/ClickHouseDB/status/2095640541858078755):

![](/_next/image?url=%2Fuploads%2Fvera_oct2026_clickbench_tweet_2f8f39ce6f.png&w=2048&q=75)

NVIDIA Vera immediately made it to the top of the ClickBench hardware leaderboard by a wide margin.

Any database engineer will admit that Vera is a truly impressive platform. It features 88 NVIDIA Olympus cores which are fully Arm\-v9\.2\-compatible. In a two socket system, this results in a total of 176 cores with 352 hardware threads thanks to NVIDIA Spatial Multithreading. Each core has access to 96/64 kB L1 data/instruction cache, 2 MB L2 cache, and 164 MB shared L3 cache. The maximum memory bandwidth ranges from 1\.0 TB/s to 1\.2 TB/s, depending on the amount of DRAM capacity in the system.

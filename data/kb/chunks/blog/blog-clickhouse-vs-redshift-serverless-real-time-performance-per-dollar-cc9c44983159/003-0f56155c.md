Both systems used their recommended real\-time ingestion paths and native preparation features: ClickHouse asynchronous inserts; Redshift streaming ingestion from Amazon MSK, and incremental materialized views. CostBench streamed **113\.2 billion stock\-market quotes** at a target of **1 million rows per second**, with equivalent sorting\-keys and daily pre\-aggregations by stock symbol. **Throughout ingestion, four interactive aggregate queries ran every ten minutes; two drill\-down queries ran every hour.** The Redshift comparison uses its live streaming view for drill\-downs and the last completed daily\-MV refresh for aggregation. Query\-result caching was disabled.

CostBench calculates its **end\-to\-end performance\-per\-dollar score** using the formula shown below: **Score \= (① preparation cost \+ ② normalized query cost) × ③ accumulated query runtime.**

Preparation cost covers the complete fresh\-data path. Normalized query cost allocates serving resources to the accepted queries; accumulated query runtime is the sum of their durations. **Lower scores are better.** The folded notes explain the writer\-capacity model, read\-path choice, and summary freshness.

**Shared harness:**

Both systems used the same source dataset, analytical workload, target\-rate model, query schedule, and result format. Destination\-specific adapters handled delivery and timing. [Part 1](https://clickhouse.com/blog/costbench-real-time-performance-per-dollar) explains the shared methodology; the [Redshift architecture](https://github.com/ClickHouse/CostBench/blob/main/full-path-realtime/quotes/redshift-serverless/README.md) records this provider’s implementation.

**Dataset and preparation:**

The complete [NBBO dataset](https://github.com/ClickHouse/CostBench/blob/main/full-path-realtime/quotes#dataset) contains 113,219,565,734 narrow, 12\-column rows. The raw\-data ordering columns were (sym, t), stock symbol and event timestamp; daily summaries used (sym, day), stock symbol and UTC day, maintaining counts, sums, and price minima and maxima.

**Continuous workload:**

Fresh events were paced toward 1 million per second while aggregate and drill\-down queries ran on their ten\-minute and hourly schedules. This models data generated continuously and prepared as it arrives. Redshift’s aggregate queries read the last completed daily\-MV refresh; aligning ingestion progress does not make that snapshot equally fresh to ClickHouse’s insert\-time summaries.

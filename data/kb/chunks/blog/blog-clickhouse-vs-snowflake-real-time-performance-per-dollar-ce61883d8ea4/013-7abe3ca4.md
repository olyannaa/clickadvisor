WHAT DOES SNOWFLAKE’S SERVING CACHE CHANGE? (click to expand) **EARLIER DIAGNOSTIC EVIDENCE**

Snowflake documents [cache warming](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/interactive#adding-an-interactive-table-to-an-interactive-warehouse) as a non\-blocking background process. [Earlier query profiles](https://github.com/ClickHouse/CostBench/blob/main/full-path-realtime/quotes/snowflake/results/t2/query_stats_drilldowns.csv) for this workload reported zero or negligible remote reads during active ingestion and a median 100% of bytes served from cache. At roughly 75 MB/s of ingest, the queried working set fit within Small’s approximately [600 GB cache](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/interactive#choosing-a-size-for-an-interactive-warehouse) and warming kept pace. These diagnostics describe the earlier configuration, rather than separately measuring cache hits for this post’s headline run.

**WHAT THAT SHOWS**

The earlier diagnostic represented a favorable cache case. Having the relevant data local still leaves the work of reading and filtering the growing raw table.

**WORKING\-SET CAPACITY**

Even the largest Interactive Warehouse, 4X\-Large, has a finite documented [44 TB cache](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/interactive#choosing-a-size-for-an-interactive-warehouse). A working set that exceeds cache capacity requires more remote reads. Warehouse sizing therefore affects both cache capacity and cost.

**WARMING THROUGHPUT**

Snowflake documents roughly [300–400 MB/s](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/interactive#resuming-and-suspending-an-interactive-warehouse) of warming throughput even for X\-Small, with larger warehouses warming faster. That exceeds the earlier workload’s roughly 75 MB/s ingest rate. At higher source rates, recently arrived data may require remote reads even when the total working set still fits.

**SIZING IMPLICATION**

The Interactive Warehouse must be sized for both the data queries touch and the rate at which new data arrives. ClickHouse’s query path reads the ordered MergeTree table without a separately populated Interactive Warehouse serving cache.

> Across the drill\-down workload, ClickHouse Cloud was 1\.48× faster overall.

### Interactive aggregations over ordered pre\-aggregated data \#

Daily pre\-aggregations let [A1–A4](https://github.com/ClickHouse/CostBench/blob/main/full-path-realtime/quotes/clickhouse-cloud/queries_mv.sql) combine prepared counts, sums, and price minima and maxima. A1–A2 filter the ordered summaries by symbol; A3–A4 read across all symbols. ClickHouse reads current summaries; Snowflake must also include newer rows.

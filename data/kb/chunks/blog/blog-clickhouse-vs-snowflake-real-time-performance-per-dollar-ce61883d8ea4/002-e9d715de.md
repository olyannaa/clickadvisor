Real\-time systems vary dramatically in the cost and efficiency of making incoming data query\-ready.
**In this series, we trace the path from data arrival to current answers, showing how it shapes preparation cost, query cost, and query runtime.**

## From fresh data to fast answers \#

[Real\-time analytics](https://clickhouse.com/blog/selecting-a-real-time-analytical-database) has to stay fast while fresh data keeps arriving. **[CostBench](https://clickhouse.com/blog/costbench-data-warehouse-cost-performance) tests this directly: queries run throughout continuous ingestion, and their answers must include newly arrived data.** Efficient preparation lowers the cost of keeping that data [query\-ready](https://clickhouse.com/blog/costbench-real-time-performance-per-dollar#what-makes-data-query-ready) and leaves less work for queries, reducing their cost and runtime.

Columnar storage lets queries skip unused fields; ordering lets drill\-downs skip unrelated quotes; current pre\-aggregations let interactive aggregations combine prepared summaries. The animation below shows how this [fresh\-data path](https://clickhouse.com/blog/costbench-real-time-performance-per-dollar#real-time-systems-must-make-fresh-data-query-ready-as-it-arrives) changes the scanning and aggregation left for queries as preparation catches up or falls behind.

CostBench measures ① preparation cost, ② query cost, and ③ runtime, then combines them into one [end\-to\-end performance\-per\-dollar score](https://clickhouse.com/blog/costbench-real-time-performance-per-dollar#turning-runtime-and-cost-into-performance-per-dollar).

This article follows those three effects through the ClickHouse–Snowflake comparison, from preparing incoming data to serving current answers.

## The workload and the result \#

> ClickHouse Cloud delivered 412× better end\-to\-end performance per dollar than Snowflake in this continuous\-ingestion workload.

Estimated monthly storage charges for raw data and daily summaries together are $12\.43 for ClickHouse Cloud, $14\.61 for Snowflake, $15\.16 for Databricks, $20\.06 for BigQuery, and $90\.44 for Redshift Serverless. These monthly charges also help explain why [CostBench excludes database storage from its end\-to\-end score](https://clickhouse.com/blog/costbench-real-time-performance-per-dollar#turning-runtime-and-cost-into-performance-per-dollar). Over the benchmark’s short duration, storage adds little compared with the combined cost of ingesting and preparing fresh data and serving queries. Storage costs become more significant over longer retention periods.

Compression reduces the bytes needed to read raw history; pre\-aggregation reduces the rows needed to answer recurring analytical questions.

**Ingestion CPU work breakdown:**

**Parsing** accounts for the largest share of ingestion CPU work; **sorting** uses roughly 19% **and pre\-aggregation** about 3%. The chart below separates parsing, sorting, pre\-aggregation, **compression**, and other ingestion work. The [client](https://github.com/ClickHouse/CostBench/blob/main/full-path-realtime/quotes/clickhouse-cloud/ingest_parquet_rows.py) sent CSV\-encoded batches through asynchronous inserts; parsing converts them into ClickHouse’s typed columnar data. “**Other ingestion work**” includes building the sparse primary index and part metadata, as well as [column statistics](https://clickhouse.com/docs/reference/engines/table-engines/mergetree-family/mergetree#column-statistics) and [secondary indexes](https://clickhouse.com/docs/concepts/best-practices/using-data-skipping-indices) when configured. This chart covers ingestion work; background merging is shown separately below.

**Reducing server\-side parsing:**

ClickHouse supports [70\+ input formats](https://clickhouse.com/docs/reference/formats), including Native, whose client\-prepared columnar blocks reduce server\-side parsing and conversion. Our [FastFormats study](https://clickhouse.com/blog/clickhouse-input-format-matchup-which-is-fastest-most-efficient) and [interactive dashboard](https://fastformats.clickhouse.com/) explore the effects of format, batching, and compression.

**Background consolidation and continued pre\-aggregation:**

The same four cores merged several million rows per second. These merges consolidated already\-ordered raw\-data parts and continued combining the summaries’ aggregation states across batches, reducing the work left for queries. The maximum active\-part count per partition stayed around 100 as the dataset grew.

**Resource usage:**

CPU usage stayed around 3\.1 of the four available cores, and tracked memory remained within the service’s 16 GiB capacity. The $28\.69 preparation cost includes both nodes and their background work.

## Unmatched end\-to\-end performance per dollar \#

CostBench’s [end\-to\-end score](https://clickhouse.com/blog/costbench-real-time-performance-per-dollar#turning-runtime-and-cost-into-performance-per-dollar) combines the ① cost of preparing fresh data with ② query\-serving cost, then multiplies that total by ③ accumulated query runtime. Lower is better: efficient preparation and fast query execution both improve the result.

ClickHouse Cloud delivered **412×** better end\-to\-end performance per dollar than [Snowflake](https://clickhouse.com/blog/clickhouse-vs-snowflake-real-time-performance-per-dollar), **752×** than [Databricks](https://clickhouse.com/blog/clickhouse-vs-databricks-real-time-performance-per-dollar), **438×** than [BigQuery Capacity](https://clickhouse.com/blog/clickhouse-vs-bigquery-real-time-performance-per-dollar), and **1,996×** than [Redshift Serverless](https://clickhouse.com/blog/clickhouse-vs-redshift-serverless-real-time-performance-per-dollar).

How we kept the comparison fair (click to expand)

**Same workload and preparation requirements.** All systems received the same data and ran the same queries on the same schedule. Raw data and daily pre\-aggregations used matching sorting or clustering keys.

**Query compute: align where possible.** Some platforms allocate compute dynamically. Configurable sizes may differ in CPU count, or provide different amounts of RAM at the same core count. We aligned query compute wherever a meaningful match was possible.

**Ingestion and preparation: minimize cost where possible.** For configurable services, we used the smallest tested setup that sustained the target rate during calibration while keeping data query\-ready. Where the platform controlled ingestion or maintenance compute, its native allocation and charges remained part of the comparison.

**Compare what that spending delivers.** Additional query compute can buy faster answers. Spending on continuous preparation can leave less work for queries, reducing their runtime and cost. CostBench ranks systems by their **end\-to\-end performance per dollar** using a score that automatically weighs the resulting query speed against the combined preparation and query\-serving cost. A more expensive setup must deliver enough faster answers to justify its higher cost.

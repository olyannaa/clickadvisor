A place to learn C\+\+ development. Many people are looking for repositories representing the frontier of software engineering, and today ClickHouse is one of the most popular open source repositories in C\+\+, where everyone can learn both the exciting stuff (C\+\+23\) and boring stuff (build systems, continuous integration and testing, code review practices, and [AI](https://clickhouse.com/blog/agentic-coding)).

A place for experiments on data structures and performance optimization. You can open a pull request as an experiment, without aiming for it to be merged \- it will be tested with the same level of scrutiny as production releases. Found a new [memory allocator](https://github.com/ClickHouse/ClickHouse/pull/11590), a new [compression library](https://github.com/ClickHouse/ClickHouse/pull/17847), a new hash table, a data format, or a [sorting algorithm](https://github.com/ClickHouse/ClickHouse/pull/16825)? \- bring it to ClickHouse, and it will expose it inside\-out. The roadmap also includes a section about [experimental](https://github.com/ClickHouse/ClickHouse/pull/100277), [weird](https://github.com/ClickHouse/ClickHouse/pull/74691#issuecomment-4585785205), and even [ridiculous](https://github.com/ClickHouse/ClickHouse/pull/99496) things.

**Where you can be proud of your work.** ClickHouse credits every contributor in the [changelog](https://github.com/ClickHouse/ClickHouse/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md) and even inside the database in the [system.contributors](https://play.clickhouse.com/play?user=play#U0VMRUNUICogRlJPTSBzeXN0ZW0uY29udHJpYnV0b3Jz) table! There are countless cases when a contributor sends an initial, incomplete implementation of a feature, and we help to finish it together. Even if the code has to be entirely rewritten, we do it proactively and take the responsibility for that, and always credit the initial author, because we care about your use case and the initial intent that made it happen. To put it simply, we love our contributors.

### Prototypes and first commits \#

The [first commit](https://github.com/ClickHouse/ClickHouse/commit/2ac2a10ff82fb8dfd46070beb83f4866bfce3b20) in ClickHouse was made on May 29, 2009, and it was a performance optimization (a replacement of libc functions `localtime`, `mktime`, `gmtime`, which were extremely slow and annoyed me by showing up in the profiler). But it was before ClickHouse existed.

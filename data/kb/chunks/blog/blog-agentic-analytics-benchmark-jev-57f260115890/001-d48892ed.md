# The Agentic Analytics Benchmark: ranking tables and labeling failures with Jev

![eduardo headshot](/_next/image?url=%2Fuploads%2Feduardo_headshot_12b9d53e91.jpeg&w=96&q=75)![lio headshot singapore](/_next/image?url=%2Fuploads%2Flio_headshot_singapore_7cc9852011.jpg&w=96&q=75)![Al Brown](/_next/image?url=%2Fuploads%2Fal_brown_headshot_09ae0cbce6.jpg&w=96&q=75)[Eduardo Vellasques](/authors/eduardo-vellasques), [Lionel Palacin](/authors/lionel-palacin) and [Al Brown](/authors/al-brown)Oct 27, 2026 · 20 minutes readThis post is about Jev, TypeSafe's System One model, and what it did for us on the Agentic Analytics Benchmark. Jev does not generate text. You hand it some state and typed questions (a yes/no answered as a calibrated probability, a pick\-one choice, a rubric score), and it returns typed answers with confidence in roughly 70 to 500 milliseconds. One call can carry many questions, and nothing in the call is specific to a benchmark: the state is whatever you pass in, and the questions are plain instructions. That makes it a classifier over whatever you hand it, and we gave it two jobs: reading a failed run and saying what went wrong, and reading a question and saying which tables it needs.

## TL;DR \#

- Jev labels every failure in under a second. On a four\-model development run, the finer taxonomy makes wrong data selection the largest bucket, most of it entity resolution rather than wrong table, and flags 18 answers the grader likely scored wrong.
- Jev also ranks the warehouse's tables per question at nDCG@10 0\.951 in 0\.4 seconds, with no fine\-tuning and no gold data at run time.
- Fed back to the agent as an in\-loop hint, the ranking left accuracy flat across 20 models, and the turn saving on the development set did not survive the board. The ranking is the result; the hint is not.
